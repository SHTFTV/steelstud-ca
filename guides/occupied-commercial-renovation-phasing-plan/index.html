Occupied Commercial Renovations: A Practical Phasing Plan

Renovating an operating office, store or strata property requires a plan that protects occupants, keeps essential routes open and creates clean handoffs between phases.

Define what must remain operational

List occupied rooms, public entrances, accessible routes, washrooms, loading areas, alarms and building services that cannot be interrupted. Confirm permitted hours and notice requirements with the property manager before promising a schedule.

Divide the work into complete zones

A useful phase has a clear boundary and can be protected, built, inspected and returned to service. Avoid opening several areas at once when labour, deliveries or inspections cannot support them. Temporary partitions and directional signs should match each phase.

Plan dust, noise and deliveries

Use containment and extraction appropriate to the work. Schedule cutting and fastening during approved periods, protect elevators and corridors, and define where materials and debris can move without crossing customers or residents.

Coordinate shutdowns and inspections

Electrical, sprinkler, fire-alarm and mechanical interruptions need approval and advance notice. Book inspections around the phased sequence so completed work is not left open longer than necessary and following trades are not blocked.

Turn over each phase deliberately

  • Complete deficiencies and cleaning before moving barriers.
  • Confirm life-safety systems and routes are restored.
  • Record hidden conditions and approved changes.
  • Brief the property contact on completed work.
  • Issue clear notice of the next phase and its impacts.

Some activities may still require after-hours work or temporary closure. The schedule should include notices, protection, deliveries, noisy work, shutdowns, inspections and cleaning—not only trade durations.
