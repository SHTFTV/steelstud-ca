Vestibule openings: review the frame before boarding

The opening is where several decisions meet. Two archive views from Burnaby General Hospital offer a useful way to organise that review while the steel frame is still visible.

SteelStud.ca editorial · Published November 2, 2026
Project identification and photographs: Mason McKenzie / Rambo Wall & Ceiling Limited.

Read the actual project

A doorway, an adjoining wall and an overhead frame

The first photograph shows the framed opening and the framing above it. The second looks into the overhead members from another angle. Neither photograph supplies dimensions or the approved assembly specification.

Start with the opening schedule

Begin the review at the doorway rather than with a count of studs. Set the current plan beside the opening schedule and the relevant wall detail. Identify which opening is being reviewed and confirm that everyone is using the same drawing revision. A photograph can show the relationship between parts; it cannot tell a bidder the required rough opening.

For a new vestibule, ask which dimensions refer to the frame and which refer to the finished clear opening. Record the intended floor finish and the wall build-up on each side. These are questions for the project documents and responsible designer, not measurements to estimate from Mason’s photographs.

This is also the point to identify who supplies the door frame and who confirms its installation requirements. Ask for unresolved items to be marked clearly in the quotation so separate trades do not price different assumptions.

01 / Follow the opening upward

The frame above a doorway deserves its own view.

Ask what belongs above the frame

The upward view reveals members crossing the vestibule and the connection between the overhead area and adjoining wall lines. Use it to ask for the specified opening-head detail and the intended relationship to the finished ceiling.

Keep the structural and installation questions with the people responsible for answering them. Stud size, thickness, spacing, fastening and support cannot be established from appearance alone. The same applies to movement allowances, any required backing and the loads associated with the selected door hardware.

Record which trade provides each required item. If services or equipment need access later, have their requirements resolved before covering this part of the work. Nothing in the archive establishes a tested fire, acoustic or other performance rating.

02 / Make the handoff reviewable

Give the next trade a specific record of decisions.

Three records to bring to the review

A useful review produces a short set of references that the framer, door supplier and drywall installer can all follow. The diagram below is an editorial planning aid, not a drawing of the photographed assembly.

  1. Opening referencePlan revision, opening identifier and the matching door/frame schedule.
  2. Assembly detailSpecified wall and head details, finish build-up and outstanding design answers.
  3. Scope handoffWho supplies the frame, backing and hardware provisions, and who records close-in checks.

Before boarding, compare those references with the actual frame and record anything that still needs an answer. A dated photograph can help explain a question, but it does not replace a required inspection or approval. Keep the inspection requirements and the responsibility for accepting concealed work explicit in the project process.

For quotation requests, send the drawings and a concise list of unresolved opening details to the installing contractor. Include your project city, access arrangements and intended programme. Confirm occupied-building requirements directly for your own premises; the archive does not establish the operating conditions or procedures of the pictured job.
